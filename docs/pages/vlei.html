Organisational identity, made verifiable.

GLEIF runs the Global LEI System: one 20-character Legal Entity Identifier for every legal entity, with open data behind it. The verifiable LEI carries that identifier into KERI and ACDC, so an organisation and the people who act for it can prove who they are, and anyone can check the proof back to one root of trust.

The premise

A name on a register is not yet a proof.

The LEI solved naming: one code per legal entity, checked against official registers and free to query. But a lookup only tells you an organisation exists, not that the party in front of you speaks for it.

The vLEI closes that gap. It issues the LEI as an ACDC credential to a KERI identifier, so an organisation, and the people acting for it, can present proof that checks back to GLEIF.

Identify the entity. Verify the authority.

Walk the chain of trust

The LEI

A 20-character code from an accredited LEI issuer, renewed each year and published with open reference data: legal name, addresses, registering authority, and who owns whom.

  • ISO 17442
  • Who is who
  • Who owns whom

The vLEI

The Legal Entity vLEI Credential is an ACDC issued by a Qualified vLEI Issuer to the organisation's own KERI identifier, carrying its LEI. Anchored in key event logs, revocable, and no blockchain required.

  • ISO 17442-3
  • KERI AID
  • ACDC

Role credentials

Official Organisational Role credentials bind a named person to a registered role, such as director or CFO. Engagement Context Role credentials cover roles the organisation defines for itself.

  • OOR
  • ECR
  • ISO 5009

The trust layer

One root, a chain of delegation.

Every vLEI resolves back to GLEIF. The vLEI Ecosystem Governance Framework, a Trust over IP Layer Four framework now at version 4.0, sets who may issue what, under which identity checks, and how each link is verified.

  1. 01 · Root

    GLEIF root of trust

    GLEIF's root AID is a multi-signature KERI identifier that stays offline and issues nothing itself. It delegates to a GLEIF External AID, which issues credentials to qualified issuers.

    Introducing the vLEI
  2. 02 · QVI

    Qualified vLEI Issuers

    Organisations that pass GLEIF's Qualification Program receive a QVI vLEI Credential and a TrustMark, and may then issue, verify and revoke vLEI credentials for legal entities.

    List of QVIs
  3. 03 · Legal entity

    Legal Entity vLEI

    The QVI checks the entity's LEI and the identities of its authorised representatives, then issues the Legal Entity vLEI Credential to the entity's own AID.

    LEI search
  4. 04 · Roles

    OOR and ECR

    The entity authorises role credentials for its people with an AUTH credential to its QVI. OOR credentials come from the QVI; ECR credentials can also be issued by the entity directly.

    AUTH credential framework

The vLEI

Six credentials, linked by edges.

Each vLEI credential is an ACDC that points to the credential that authorised it, so a verifier can walk any signature back to GLEIF.

Credentials

What gets issued, by whom.

Defined in the vLEI Ecosystem Governance Framework v4.0. The schemas are public, and every issuance is anchored in the issuer's key event log.

  • 01 · GEDA → QVI

    QVI vLEI Credential

    Issued by GLEIF's External AID to each Qualified vLEI Issuer. The top of every chain.

  • 02 · QVI → LE

    Legal Entity vLEI Credential

    Issued by a QVI to the organisation's AID, carrying its LEI.

  • 03 · LE → QVI

    OOR AUTH Credential

    The entity's instruction to its QVI to issue an official role credential to a named person.

  • 04 · QVI → person

    OOR vLEI Credential

    Binds a person to an official role drawn from the ISO 5009 list, such as CEO or director.

  • 05 · LE → QVI

    ECR AUTH Credential

    The entity's instruction to its QVI to issue an engagement role credential.

  • 06 · QVI or LE → person

    ECR vLEI Credential

    A role the entity defines for an engagement, such as a supplier contact or a project signatory.

Under the hood

Built on KERI, not a ledger.

The vLEI uses Trust over IP specifications for identifiers, credentials and encoding, and was standardised as ISO 17442-3 in 2024. GLEIF runs a witness network instead of a blockchain.

  • 01

    KERI

    Key Event Receipt Infrastructure. Each identifier is self-certifying, with a key event log that witnesses receipt. Keys rotate by pre-rotation.

  • 02

    ACDC

    Authentic Chained Data Containers. Credentials that chain to other credentials through edges, with issuance and revocation tracked in a transaction event log.

  • 03

    CESR

    Composable Event Streaming Representation. One encoding for keys, signatures and events, in text or binary.

  • 04

    Multi-signature control

    GLEIF's root has been documented at a three-of-seven signing threshold and its External AID at two-of-five. QVIs also control their AIDs as groups.

In use

Where the vLEI is working.

From signed reports to trade documents and onchain wallets.

  • Reporting

    Signed annual reports

    GLEIF signed its 2021 annual report with vLEIs: the CFO and auditors signed the financial statements, the CEO and board chair the whole report.

  • Trade

    Electronic bills of lading

    GLEIF and AEOTrade are working on vLEI-backed eBLs. Sydney's Bloom Fintech took its Legal Entity vLEI through TradeGo, a Singapore QVI, in September 2026.

  • Onchain

    Wallets and smart contracts

    GLEIF and Chainlink are combining the vLEI with Cross-Chain Identity and the Automated Compliance Engine to prove the entity behind a wallet or contract.

  • Telecoms

    Verified communications

    Provenant, the first QVI qualified by GLEIF, is bringing the vLEI to communications networks.

The LEI

Who is who, and who owns whom.

The code is the small part. Around it sits a governed system that keeps more than three million records current, checked and free to use.

Governance

Three tiers, held apart.

Policy, operation and issuance sit with different bodies, so no single party owns the identifier.

  • 01 · Policy

    Regulatory Oversight Committee

    The LEI ROC: public authorities from around the world that set policy for the Global LEI System.

  • 02 · Operation

    GLEIF

    A not-for-profit Swiss foundation in Basel, set up by the Financial Stability Board in 2014. It accredits issuers and publishes the data.

  • 03 · Issuance

    LEI issuers

    Accredited Local Operating Units that register, check and renew LEIs. Around 40 operate today.

  • 04 · Data

    Open data

    The full dataset, the Golden Copy, is free to download and query by API. GLEIF measures and publishes its data quality every month.

The code

What the 20 characters carry.

No embedded meaning: the code points to the record, and the record carries the facts.

  • 1–4

    Issuer prefix

    Identifies the LEI issuer that registered the code, keeping codes unique across issuers.

  • 5–18

    Entity part

    Assigned by the issuer. It says nothing about the entity on its own.

  • 19–20

    Check digits

    Two digits under ISO 7064 MOD 97-10 that catch most typing errors.

  • Levels 1 and 2

    Reference data

    Level 1 is who is who: legal name, addresses, registration. Level 2 is who owns whom: direct and ultimate parents.

Momentum

Where the LEI is spreading.

Regulation keeps widening the LEI's reach beyond capital markets.

  • Growth

    Past three million

    More than 355,000 LEIs were issued in 2025, annual growth of 13.5%. The active population passed three million in the first quarter of 2026.

  • United States

    Financial Data Transparency Act

    The joint data standards rule names the LEI as the common legal entity identifier. It took effect on 1 October 2026, with agency rules to follow within two years.

  • European Union

    Business wallets and AML

    GLEIF is arguing for the LEI to anchor European Business Wallets, ahead of the EU Anti-Money Laundering Regulation applying from 10 July 2027.

  • Cross-border

    Project Aperta

    The BIS Innovation Hub Hong Kong Centre used the LEI to streamline KYB and AML checks for SMEs across linked open finance networks.
